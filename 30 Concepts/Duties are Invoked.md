# Duties are Invoked

## Definition (as held)
What distinguishes a duty from a value is not its content but that a **counterparty can invoke it**. A value is held; a duty is owed to someone with standing to call it in. Invocation is what makes duties binding, and it is why duties scale where values cannot.

## The binding argument
> **You don't get to opt out of the consequences of acting on others by declining the framework.**

The claim is not authority over a non-believer's beliefs. It is about the **asymmetry of action**: the person acted upon bears the consequence whether or not they hold the philosophy, so the actor declining to hold it changes nothing about what is owed. → [[OQ-13 Enumerate the Duties]], [[Part 4 — Duties, the Philosophical Constitution]]

> [!claude]
> A duty nobody can invoke is a value wearing a coat. This is the test for the candidate duty list: for each one, name who can invoke it and what invoking it looks like. Several of the current candidates fail that test as written.
