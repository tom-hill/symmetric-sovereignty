# Reversibility and Exit

## Definition (as held)
A **load-bearing structural principle**: the legitimacy of any exercise of sovereignty over another depends on whether the other can leave or undo. Identified as load-bearing because it **arrived independently from five directions** in dialogue — the framework's own test for structural importance ([[Convergence as Evidence]]).

## The five directions (reconstructed — Tom to confirm)
1. The VC critique — funding that removes founders' exit ([[Part 8 — The Model for Modern Business is Broken]])
2. [[Commitment without Rigidity]] — holding a goal while able to let it go
3. [[Vulnerability without Collapse]] — openness without being trapped by it
4. The duty layer's need for a first duty — *do not trap* ([[Values vs Duties]])
5. The Reyor practice — making change survivable = not cutting off retreat ([[Reyor]])

Also present in [[Proactive Reactivity]] ("safety nets") and in the Reyor & H+G heuristic "sort decisions by reversibility, not importance".
