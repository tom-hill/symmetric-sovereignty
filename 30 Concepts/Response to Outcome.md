# Response to Outcome

## Definition (Tom, 2026-09-30)

> [!question] Tom
> Judge people not on the perceptions of what they do, or what they say they intended, but on the way they react to the outcomes of their actions, even the ones they did not intend. That is where someone's true character and nature is observed.

The criterion for judging a person is neither **intention** (which only they can report, and which is the cheapest thing to claim) nor **perceived action** (which the observer reconstructs, usually badly, and usually from an account rather than from the thing itself). It is what they do **once the consequences arrive**, including consequences they did not mean and could not have foreseen.

## Why it earns a note
It is the first criterion in the framework that is **observable by a third party and cheap to apply**, which makes it usable where most of the philosophy is not. It also solves a problem the vault had left open: [[Condemnation]] is defined as what happens when a person becomes a case, but nothing said what judging them *properly* would look like. This does.

## Where it came from
Written while examining his own failure to apply it. → [[What is justice?]]

## Neighbours
- [[Attribution not Blame]] — *naming the cause and owning the response are both required*. This is the same principle turned outward: attribution is how you handle your own causation, response-to-outcome is how you assess someone else's.
- [[Struggle Revealed Through Experience]] — prefer observed behaviour to self-report. The same instinct, extended from struggle to character.
- [[Genuine Wants]] — a want that needs no self-persuasion. Both are instruments for getting past what a person claims about themselves.
- [[Condemnation]] · [[Kindness]] · [[Duties are Invoked]]

> [!claude]
> Two edges worth testing before this is relied on.
>
> It requires **time**. You cannot assess a response to an outcome that has not landed yet, which means the criterion is unavailable in exactly the moment judgement is usually demanded. That may be the point rather than a defect: it is an argument for waiting, and it is precisely the waiting that was not done here.
>
> And it can be **gamed** more easily than intent can. A visible, well-managed response to a consequence is a performance anyone with resources can stage, and the people best placed to stage it are the people with the most power. The framework would then be judging presentation and calling it character. [[Earned Authority]] has the same exposure.
