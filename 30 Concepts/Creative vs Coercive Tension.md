# Creative vs Coercive Tension

## The problem it answers
If tension is the generative condition and conflict is part of the natural order, does the framework have any internal resource to say *this particular conflict is wrong*? Without one, embracing tension risks licensing bad actors — which is the worry Tom opened the morality session with.

## Definition (as held — provisional)
**Tension as condition** is unavoidable and productive: the automatic result of real forces acting on a system. **Tension as permission** is the misreading in which anything goes because everything is tension. The framework holds the first.

The distinction that does the work is between tension that **expands the range of lives available** and tension that **narrows it** — the same criterion that separates germ theory and abolition from cultural flattening. Tom's own line is drawn at *forceful deliberate harm to others*, defended as the point at which one agent's agency extinguishes another's.

## Why it is only a seed
> [!claude]
> This distinction is asserted in dialogue and developed nowhere. It is load-bearing for [[Part 1 — Tension as the Generative Condition]] — which currently claims resolution always produces imbalance, without distinguishing the tensions that should be resolved. Part 1's own Claude callout flags the same gap from the other side, via [[Shadow (Inversion)]]: cruelty is not a counterpart to strength, it is strength inverted. Counterpart / shadow and creative / coercive may be the same cut made twice.
