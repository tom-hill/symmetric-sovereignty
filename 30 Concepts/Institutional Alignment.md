# Institutional Alignment

## Definition (as held)
The framework's principles turning up **already institutionalised**, in societies that did not derive them from it. Three arrivals, each from a different tradition:

- **The German *Mittelstand*** — generational, family-owned businesses built across decades, with cultural respect for process and craft, long-term horizons over quarterly returns, and earned expertise over positional authority. A standing answer to the VC critique that does not require the critique. → [[Part 8 — The Model for Modern Business is Broken]]
- **Dutch *verzuiling*** (pillarisation) — a political system built to **hold competing worldviews in productive tension** rather than resolve them into consensus. [[Tension]] as constitutional design.
- **Māori *mana*** — dignity and authority accrued through action and service rather than position. [[Earned Authority]], arrived at entirely outside the Western lineage.

## Why it matters
This is the best external evidence the framework has for the **scale-invariance** reading of its own name: the same principle holding at institutional and civilisational scale, in places that never read it. → [[OQ-11 The Name]], [[Convergence as Evidence]], [[Scales]]

## The disconfirming case, which matters more
**Scandinavia and the Jante Law** — the cultural pressure that says *don't think you're better or different*. Nordic societies score highly on nearly every outcome this framework says it wants, and achieve it by suppressing exactly the variance and individual difference the framework says are indispensable.

> [!claude]
> This is the framework's cleanest falsification test and it should not be filed as an exception. Either variance is not necessary for flourishing, or "flourishing" means something other than what those societies optimise for — and saying which is a real commitment, not a hedge. It also bears directly on [[Variance and the Average That Doesn't Exist]], which predicts a homogeneous society can absorb near-unlimited standardisation without harm. The Nordic case may be that prediction coming true, and it looks like a point *against* the manifesto rather than for it.

## The honest summary
No country has fully institutionalised this philosophy, because the philosophy sits in genuine tension itself: individualist but not libertarian, long-termist but not conservative, process-oriented but not bureaucratic, strength-celebrating but anti-domination.
