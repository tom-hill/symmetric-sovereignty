# Variance and the Average That Doesn't Exist

## Definition (as held)
The harm done by normalising a population scales not with the **size** of that population but with its **internal variance**. Normalising across a homogeneous group of ten thousand costs almost nothing. Normalising across a heterogeneous group of two hundred destroys something. The dose is *scale relative to internal difference*.

## Origin
Tom's own line, in the localisation argument: as standardisation spreads "we trend toward an average/baseline that doesn't really exist in many if any localised area." The Land Rover on the farm road is the worked case — a vehicle fitted to actual terrain rather than to the mean of all terrain.

## Why it matters
It explains, without appeal to taste, why globalisation of **trade** is tolerable and globalisation of **culture** is not: culture is where the variance was. → [[OQ-14 Normalisation as the Only Unheld Tension]]

> [!claude]
> This is the most quantitative claim anywhere in the framework and the easiest to test against real data. It is also the one most likely to be wrong in an interesting way — it predicts that a highly homogeneous society should be able to absorb almost unlimited standardisation without harm, which is close to the case *for* the Nordic model that [[OQ-15 The Hidden Premise About Human Nature]] is suspicious of.
