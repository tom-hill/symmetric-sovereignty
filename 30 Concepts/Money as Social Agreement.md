# Money as Social Agreement

## Definition (as held)
Money is not a fixed stock of a thing but a **standing social agreement about claims**. Around 95–97% of money in circulation is created by commercial banks at the moment they make loans. One person's debt is another person's savings, which is why debt cannot simply be cancelled — cancelling the record destroys the savings on the other side, and at international level robs the country that exported real goods and took IOUs in return.

The operative consequences:
- **Taxes remove money from the economy; spending injects it.** A government running a perfect surplus is by definition extracting more from the private sector than it puts in, and private savings shrink.
- Government deficits during downturns are often the **stabiliser**, not the problem.
- **The real constraint is not money. It is real resources — and that includes people.** → [[Real Resources Constraint]]
- Debt is not inert: it is collateral for further credit, so the system is layered claims on claims. Unwinding it is pulling the keystone from an arch, not pressing delete.

## Why it belongs in this philosophy
It is [[Normalisation]] at the civilisational scale, working on a single scalar. The household-budget metaphor is a norm authored elsewhere, imposed, unexitable, and it has displaced the capacity to think about public investment at all — which makes it a case study for [[Foreclosure]] and [[Authorship vs Imposition]] rather than a detour into economics. It also supplies the mechanism behind [[OQ-08 Why the Obsession]]: a single number is the ultimate flattening.
