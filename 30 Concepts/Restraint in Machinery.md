# Restraint in Machinery

## Definition (as held)
At any scale above the personal, restraint has to be **built into structures rather than into people**. Shared values do not scale — values are self-administered by definition, and cannot be shared into anyone. A constitution is restraint in machinery; so is a right of exit, a term limit, a revocation clause.

## Origin
In the five-challenges session Tom reached for *shared values* as the mechanism for scaling the philosophy past the individual. That concedes the case, since it requires the very thing values cannot do. The correction was structural.

## Why it matters
It is the strongest argument that the duty layer must be **enumerable, minimal and enforceable** rather than aspirational — a constitution is short, and it works by machinery. It also explains why [[Reversibility and Exit]] recurs: exit is the most portable piece of restraint-in-machinery there is, requiring no shared belief from anyone.
