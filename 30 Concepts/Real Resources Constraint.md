# Real Resources Constraint

## Definition (as held)
The binding limit on what a society can do is not the money available but the **real resources** available — materials, energy, infrastructure, and above all *people*: their number, their health, their training, their time. Money is the accounting; resources are the thing accounted for.

## Why it matters to the framework
It converts the question "can we afford it?" into "do we have the people and the capacity, and if not, why not?" — which is a question about [[Capability-sharing vs Goods-sharing]] and about education, not about budgets. It is also the point at which the anti-normalisation argument acquires a floor: a flattened population is a **resource-poor** one, because variance is capability. → [[Variance and the Average That Doesn't Exist]]
