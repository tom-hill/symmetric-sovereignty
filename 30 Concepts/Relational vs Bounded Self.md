# Relational vs Bounded Self

## Definition (as held)
Whether the **individual** or the **relationship** is the fundamental unit of moral analysis. Western philosophy, including at its best, starts with the bounded self and extends outward. Confucian, Ubuntu and much Islamic philosophy start with relationship as the primary reality and treat the self as derivative.

This is not a preference. It produces different moral logic: in a relational ontology, harm is **rupturing a web** rather than violating an individual, and justice is **restoration of relationship** rather than restoration of rights.

## Why it matters here
Symmetric Sovereignty is built on the bounded self — [[Sovereignty]] is authority over *one's own* thinking, and the duties are owed *by* a sovereign *to* a counterparty. The systems thinking pushes against the bounded self, but the ghost of it remains in the architecture.

> **What would the framework look like if the bond, not the person, were the base unit?** The values are self-administered, which presupposes a self to administer them. The duties are relational already — which may mean the duty layer is the part of the framework that would survive the swap, and the value layer the part that would not. Tom to write.
