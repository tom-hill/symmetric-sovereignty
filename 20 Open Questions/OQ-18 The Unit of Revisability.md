# OQ-18 — At what unit must revisability be available: the individual, or the collective?

## Where it came from
Put to Tom that taxation passes the [[Foreclosure]] triad — not on exit, which is formally available and practically theoretical, but on **revisability** and **feedback path**. He rejected the case:

> [!question] Tom
> A forced collection of money, applied to all (unequally), to fund decisions made by a few, with little to no ability for **individuals** to opt out, alter, or force amendment, is not a good example of foreclosure that is ok. It's an example of imbalanced power.

He allows the same arrangement at small scale: *"If a smaller collective of people decide to contribute to a shared pot they use to do things for their localised community to serve their needs, go for it."*

## The question
The test being applied is **individual** revisability, not collective. That is a coherent position — it is the anarcho-liberal one, which Tom states as his lean. But it has a consequence.

Individual revisability is unavailable at any scale above the small collective, by arithmetic rather than by design. If it is the standard, then **no** collective decision binding non-consenters is ever legitimate.

## Why this is not an abstract problem

[[Part 11 — Money, Time and the Commons]] contains Tom's own constructive programme: a sovereign fund held at the Bank of England, removal of first-past-the-post, political remuneration tied to long-horizon outcomes, national education reform.

Every one of those is **nationally imposed, applied to all, decided by few, and not individually opt-out-able.** They are the same instrument he rejects in the taxation case, and several of them are funded by it.

> [!claude]
> This is a live contradiction between two notes in the same vault, and it is the framework's central political problem rather than an inconsistency to tidy away. The fork:
>
> - **Individual revisability** → the anarcho-liberal reading holds, and Part 11's programme is incoherent and must go.
> - **Collective revisability** → Part 11 stands, taxation is legitimate in a democracy, and the anarcho-liberal lean is a preference about *scale* rather than a principle about *consent*.
>
> There may be a third route through [[Exit Cost]], which is why that note exists. But the fork cannot be left unchosen, because [[OQ-13 Enumerate the Duties]] cannot be written without it: whether a duty binds a non-consenter *is* this question.

## Note on the small-collective case
The shared village pot is still forced on any dissenting member of that collective — unless they can leave cheaply. So exit returns at small scale, and Tom's position may reduce to: **legitimacy varies inversely with the cost of leaving.** → [[Exit Cost]]
