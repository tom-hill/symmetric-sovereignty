# OQ-10 — Why is condemnation the shadow of kindness?

## The question
The framework says kindness is the mechanism that prevents values inverting into their shadows, and that **condemnation** is kindness's own shadow. Why condemnation — rather than weakness, indulgence, or sentimentality, which are the usual accusations against kindness?

## Why the choice is interesting
It implies the failure of kindness is *moralising*: kindness that has decided who deserves it. That is a much stronger and less obvious claim, and it fits the framework — condemnation is kindness that has stopped seeing the other as sovereign and started seeing them as a case.

## What it would need
- A mechanism: how does kindness become condemnation? (Candidate: when it acquires a standard — see [[Normalisation]]; or through fear — see [[OQ-06 Fear]].)
- A guard: if kindness guards the other values, what guards kindness? (Candidate: [[Reversibility and Exit]] — the condemned have no exit; kindness that leaves a way back cannot condemn.)
