# OQ-03 — Does the VC critique target mechanism or scale?

## The question
The notebook page ([[Spread B — The Model for Modern Business is Broken]]) says VC was *designed* well — to seed capital-intensive ideas — and *became* broken when it became the default. Is the objection to the **mechanism** (equity for capital, exit as the goal, LP returns as the master) or to the **scale** (a good tool for a narrow case, applied everywhere)?

## Why it matters
- If mechanism: the framework is against VC as such, and the positive model must be built without it.
- If scale: the framework is against *normalisation* of one business model — a dosage argument — and VC has a legitimate place at the right dose.
- Tom's own text supports scale ("became the default way"), but the language ("self-cannibalising", "sacrifices the sovereignty of the people who found them") is mechanism-level.

## Angles
- The [[Dosage]] reframe suggests scale. Tension: the mechanism *removes exit* for founders, and [[Reversibility and Exit]] is load-bearing — which points at mechanism.
- Possible resolution: the mechanism is fine *when reversible* (founders can leave, the company can be un-funded) and broken when it is not. Then the critique is neither mechanism nor scale but **irreversibility**.

## The counter-argument, from the transcripts

> [!claude] Recovered from the five-challenges session
> Venture capital is the one capital structure that will fund things almost guaranteed to fail. It *selects hard for weird*. What produced the homogeneity being described is arguably the **volume of capital**, not the model: too much money chasing too few outlier outcomes, so the fund pattern-matches to whatever last worked. That is a critique of scale, not of mechanism.
>
> And the reference cases do not support the alternative cleanly. **Bell Labs ran on monopoly rent. OSRD ran on wartime state funding.** Neither is a bootstrapping story. If the positive examples of held-tension institutions were all funded by concentrated, non-market capital, the critique needs to say what it is actually for — because it is not obviously bootstrapping.

This is the sharpest version of the question and it should be answered before [[Part 8 — The Model for Modern Business is Broken]] is written in the framework's own vocabulary. See also [[Bell Labs]], [[Vannevar Bush]].
