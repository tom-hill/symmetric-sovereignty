# OQ-11 — What does "Symmetric" mean, given the framework rejects symmetry?

## The question
The dialogue settled that "balance" and "symmetry" are [[Resolution Words]] — terms that smuggle in a rest state and are inconsistent with the core claim. The framework is called **Symmetric** Sovereignty.

## Readings that might survive
- **Reciprocal**, not balanced: my sovereignty is legitimate only if it preserves yours — the *symmetry* is in the duty, not in a state of rest. This is a relational symmetry (like a symmetric relation in mathematics: if A→B then B→A), not an equilibrium.
- **Symmetric under scale**: the same principle holds at every scale (individual → civilisational). A symmetry in the physicist's sense — invariance under transformation — which is *not* a resolution word; it's about what stays true when you change the frame.

> [!claude]
> Either reading rescues the name and, interestingly, both strengthen the framework: the first makes reciprocity the *definition* of legitimate sovereignty; the second makes scale-invariance a testable claim. The second is closer to Herbert and to systems thinking. But the book needs to say which, early, or every reader who reaches the "no resolution words" passage will ask this question.

## Resolution — 2026-09-16

**Symmetry of power, defined as an act rather than a state.**

Neither of the two rescues above was needed. Both were retrofits — arguments for why "symmetric" need not mean what it appears to mean. The answer arrived instead from the mechanism, in the session on [[OQ-14 Normalisation as the Only Unheld Tension]]: what makes an exercise of sovereignty legitimate is that power is symmetric enough that neither party can foreclose the other.

Asked whether "symmetric power" is not itself a [[Resolution Words|resolution word]] — a rest state, achievable and then finished — Tom:

> [!question] Tom
> It is itself tension to be held, it will be in flux, it will change its shape with time and the ebb and flow of humanity and the people occupying it at the time. It has no universal definition of "state". It is instead an **act** of holding tension between power to prevent a total collapse or overwhelm of any one group, persons, peoples or "thing".

So the title parses as *the ongoing act of holding power open*, not *balanced sovereignty*. "Symmetric" survives because it names an act, and acts cannot be rest states. Note also what the definition sets: not an equality of power but a **floor** — the prevention of overwhelm. That is [[Agency without Domination]] at civilisational scale.
