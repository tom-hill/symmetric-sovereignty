# OQ-16 — Morality across scales, and the side of this I cannot see

## The question, in Tom's words

> [!question] Tom
> I started by questioning if my push for symmetry through tension enabled bad actors. […] This then led me to question why I believe I had the moral imperative to call them terrible (my line is drawn at forceful deliberate harm to others). But the problem extends. What and who draws the line of morality, and what causes us to extend this line and apply it to all. Is the line a fixed construct, or a fluid one. Is morality at the individual level different from morality at a family, neighbourhood, societal, or international level? If so … why so? What moral emerges from the larger system that can't exist at the singular level, or what moral at the singular level can't exist at the higher?

And the sharper second half:

> [!question] Tom
> I'm also cognisant of the fact I am western, and I will carry with me my own sense of morality and ethics shaped by the world and people within. So what view, what emergence, what morality am I missing. What side to my philosophy is there that I myself may never be able to complete by the nature of who I am and where I am from.

## Why it matters
[[Sovereignty]] holds that sovereignty is legitimate only when it preserves others' sovereignty. That is a universal claim, and [[Part 7 — Scales]] currently asserts scale-invariance without testing it against cultures that do not share the framework's starting units. This is the question that decides whether the framework is a philosophy or a local dialect.

## What the session established

**What changes at scale is not the principle but the feedback loops.** Individual morality is disciplined by direct consequence — you see the person you harm. Collective morality operates with diffuse causality, long time horizons, and diffused responsibility. Institutions, laws and norms exist to *simulate at scale what direct consequence does naturally at the individual level*, and they are imperfect translations. The worked paradoxes: honesty is a virtue individually but total state transparency destabilises and kills; loyalty to family is moral but extended unconditionally to nation becomes tribalism; self-sacrifice for a child is heroic, for an abstraction it produces terrorism. → [[Feedback Loops as the Scaling Variable]]

**Tension as condition, not tension as permission.** The worry that embracing tension legitimises bad actors dissolves on that distinction — but only if the framework can say *this particular conflict is wrong*, which requires that not all tension is equal. **Creative tension differs from coercive tension**, and that distinction is not yet developed anywhere in the vault. → [[Creative vs Coercive Tension]]

## The three structural blind spots named

> [!claude]
> 1. **The relational self vs the bounded self.** Western philosophy starts with the individual as the fundamental unit; the ghost of that remains in this framework even though the systems thinking pushes against it. Confucian, Ubuntu and much Islamic philosophy start with *relationship* as primary and the self as derivative. In a relational ontology, harm is rupturing a web rather than violating an individual, and justice is restoration of relationship rather than of rights. The whole framework would look different if the unit of analysis were the bond. → [[Relational vs Bounded Self]]
> 2. **Cyclical vs progressive time.** The notebook assumes tension produces *forward* movement — growth, emergence, advancement. That is a post-Enlightenment structure. On cyclical time, tension generates *return*, and resolution is restoration rather than innovation. A philosophy oriented to return and maintenance would ask: why do you assume the tension should go anywhere? → [[Cyclical vs Progressive Time]]
> 3. **Embodied and communal knowledge.** Western epistemology privileges the propositional — things that can be stated, argued, written in notebooks. Much non-Western moral wisdom is encoded in practice, ritual and communal habit and cannot be extracted into principles without loss. So there may be moral insight this framework *structurally cannot absorb*, because the framework is built on articulation. The missing thing may not be an idea but a form of knowing.

## Where it lands

The honest position offered was **structural deference** — not relativism, not abandoning the line at forceful deliberate harm, but holding the framework as *a proposal, not a verdict*. With the named risk: systems thinkers use the elegance of the framework as a substitute for that deference, and the map starts to feel like the territory.

> [!claude]
> This connects directly to [[OQ-02 Creed to Prescription]]. "A proposal, not a verdict" is a third option alongside report and prescription, and it is more defensible than either — but it is also exactly the comfortable answer flagged in OQ-02 as a possible escape hatch. The difference between *structural deference* and *an unwillingness to claim anything* needs to be made in the text, not assumed.
