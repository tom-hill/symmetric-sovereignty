# OQ-14 — Is normalisation the only unheld tension?

## The question

> [!claude] The challenge as originally put
> Every other force in your framework gets held against its opposite. Normalisation gets named as the enemy and shown the door. But if the claim is that health lives in holding opposites, then standardisation has to be one of the poles you hold, not the thing you're at war with. Where would you actually want more of it? A hospital, a bridge, a payroll run. So the argument isn't against normalisation, it's against normalisation escaping its domain and colonising ones where variance is the point. That's a much sharper claim, and it costs you the manifesto's cleanest line.

## Tom's first position

> [!question] Tom
> I don't think normalisation is the enemy per-se, maybe this is another dosage thing. Normalisation is fine within a localised populous. But normalisation at scale is the enemy. […] Globalisation of trade and access is great, globalisation of culture and belief is not.

With the worked example: local bus routes in vehicles designed for the actual roads, farming communities using Land Rovers, doctors and food tailored to a small populace — normalised, but not on a per-person basis. As standardisation spreads we "trend toward an average/baseline that doesn't really exist in many if any localised area."

## Where it moved to

Four successive refinements, each displacing the one before:

1. **Not scale — authorship.** The Land Rover route was a norm *authored by the people it served*, arising from terrain they actually drove on. The flattened global norm is authored elsewhere. Size correlates only because past a certain scale nobody living under a norm can have written it. → [[Authorship vs Imposition]]
2. **Not headcount — variance.** Harm scales with the *variance of the population being averaged over*, not its size. Normalising across a homogeneous group of ten thousand costs almost nothing; across a heterogeneous group of two hundred it destroys something. This is what makes globalisation of *culture* worse than globalisation of *trade* — culture is where the variance was.
3. **Not variance — foreclosure.** Local norms are exitable, revisable, and carry a feedback path because you live with the consequences. A global norm has removed all three. There is no outside, so it can no longer be corrected by the people it is wrong for. **The criterion isn't size, it's foreclosure.** → [[Reversibility and Exit]]
4. **Not addition — layer.** Germ theory doesn't compete for the slot local medicine occupied; it sits *beneath* it, and local practice can be rebuilt on top. A norm that occupies the slot displaces; a norm that sits beneath the slot enables. → [[Additive vs Displacing Forces]]

## What is still soft

> [!claude]
> Three edges the session left live.
>
> **Network effects collapse the additive test.** Access to English adds to every individual who takes it and kills the language underneath. Nobody was coerced; each choice was a genuine gain. Additive at the individual level and subtractive at the system level are *the same event viewed from two distances*. So "does it add or displace" needs a timescale and a level attached, or it will approve things that hollow out.
>
> **The pipe is one pipe.** The same infrastructure that carries the vaccine carries the algorithm; cheap grain is access to food and the end of the local farm. You are not choosing between two globalisations, you are pricing a bundle. The trade-good/culture-bad framing is too clean to survive contact.
>
> **Trade isn't sharing, and this one bites the framework.** Tom's account has countries "acting like people in a global community… the sharing is the point, cooperation is the point." But trade is exchange between parties with unequal power, and the terms follow the power. The access is real and it arrived through competition and self-interest. Tom's own coherence rule — that you cannot market a philosophy of sovereignty using manufactured urgency, that the mechanism must cohere with the value — applies here too. If the good outcome came from a mechanism he would reject, either the rule bends or he owes an account of why this case is different.

## What resolving it costs

If foreclosure is the criterion, then [[Anti-normalisation]] stops being a stance and becomes a domain argument, and [[Normalisation]] stops being "the adversary". [[Part 1 — Tension as the Generative Condition]], [[Part 2 — The Values]], [[Part 6 — Normalisation as Dosage]] and [[Part 8 — The Model for Modern Business is Broken]] all move. The manifesto loses its cleanest line and gains a testable one.
