# OQ-13 — What are the duties?

## The question
The dialogue established a second layer — duties owed to a counterparty, binding on non-believers, forming a philosophical constitution — but the record contains no list of duties. This is the largest blank in the manuscript.

## Candidates implied by the material
1. Preserve the other's sovereignty — never standardise, control, or override ([[Agency without Domination]])
2. Leave an exit — never trap ([[Reversibility and Exit]])
3. Be honest about fragility to those who depend on you ([[Vulnerability without Collapse]])
4. Add without displacing ([[Additive vs Displacing Forces]])
5. Share capability, not dependency ([[Capability-sharing vs Goods-sharing]])
6. Earn authority; never claim it by rank ([[Earned Authority]])

## The binding argument, recovered

[[Part 4 — Duties, the Philosophical Constitution]] records "binding on non-believers" as an unargued claim. The five-challenges session did argue it, in a single sentence the recap called load-bearing:

> **You don't get to opt out of the consequences of acting on others by declining the framework.**

The structure that goes with it:

- **Values** are held in tension and self-administered. They cannot be prescribed, which is why they do not scale.
- **Duties** are owed to a counterparty *who can invoke them*. Invocation is what makes them binding, and it is why they scale where values cannot. → [[Duties are Invoked]]
- The binding is therefore not a claim of authority over the non-believer's beliefs. It is a claim about **the asymmetry of action**: the person acted upon bears the consequence whether or not they hold the philosophy, so the actor's declining to hold it changes nothing about what is owed.

> [!claude]
> This is option (a) from the three routes offered in Part 4 — duties bind *me* in how I treat *them* — but it is stronger than Part 4's version, because "who can invoke them" gives the counterparty standing rather than making the duty a private scruple. A duty nobody can invoke is a value wearing a coat.

## Attribution, not blame

A further principle from the same session, currently homed nowhere in the vault:

> **Naming the cause and owning the response are both required, and stating a fact is not an excuse. Bidirectional, or it becomes hustle culture.**

This reads as a duty in its own right — and possibly the one that makes the others operable, since a duty that cannot be invoked without the invoker being told they are making excuses is not invocable in practice. → [[Attribution not Blame]]

## Restraint built into machinery

On how duties survive scaling, the session recorded a concession and a correction:

> [!claude]
> Tom reached for *shared values* as the mechanism for scaling past the individual — which concedes the case, since values are self-administered by definition and cannot be shared into anyone. The answer arrived at was structural: **restraint built into machinery, not people.** That is what a constitution *is*, and it is the strongest available argument that the duty layer must be enumerable, minimal, and enforceable rather than aspirational. → [[Restraint in Machinery]]

## What Tom needs to decide
- Which of these are duties (owed, binding) vs values (held, self-administered)?
- On what basis do they bind non-believers? → [[OQ-02 Creed to Prescription]]
- Is there a *minimum* set — a constitution is short.
