# OQ-12 — Is conflict part of the natural condition, or is tension not conflict?

## The question
[[Tension without Immotion]] (notebook): "Embracing active tension, **conflict**, and change as the natural condition of human flourishing."
Dialogue (later): "**Tension is not conflict** — resolution produces imbalance; the aim is to hold tension consciously."

These may be reconcilable — conflict as tension at a scale where someone has to lose; or conflict as tension that has become *visible*. But the book will be read as contradicting itself unless the terms are fixed.

## Naming history (recovered 2026-09-16)

The transcripts show that [[Tension without Immotion]] was not the first formulation. In the earlier value set — then called the **"Hill Credo"** — the same entry read:

> **Motion over stasis**: Embracing active, balanced conflict and recognizing that *the only rational fear is motionlessness*; viewing continuous action as the ultimate state of sovereignty rather than falling into the trap of standardized, vacuous "peace".

Two things follow. First, **conflict was in the formulation from the beginning**, which means its presence in the notebook is deliberate rather than a slip. Second, the earlier version carried "balanced", which the framework later rejected as a [[Resolution Words|resolution word]] — so the rewrite that produced "Tension without Immotion" was already removing resolution vocabulary. Whether it should also have removed "conflict" is exactly this question.

> [!claude]
> A candidate resolution the naming history suggests: *tension* is the condition (neutral, automatic, always present), *conflict* is tension that has become visible because someone is bearing its cost, and *motion* is what the framework actually wants — which is why the earliest formulation named stasis rather than immotion as the enemy. If that holds, the glossary needs three terms, not two.
