# OQ-02 — When did creed become prescription, and must the content change?

## The question
The values were written as a personal creed. At some point — the notebook records it as a margin note on [[Leadership without Entitlement]]: *"phrasing mistake — this is no longer just about me!"* — they became something offered to others. Does that shift require changing only the **delivery** (pronouns, voice, framing) or the **content** (what is actually claimed)?

## Why it matters
- It is the difference between a memoir with principles and a philosophy.
- Tom is explicitly attentive to the distinction between *reporting from one life* and *prescribing to others*.
- It determines what the duty layer can claim: prescriptions that bind non-believers need a justification a creed never needed. → [[OQ-13 Enumerate the Duties]]

## Angles in the material
- The [[Inductive Method]] offers a possible answer: the content stays *reported*, and the prescription is only "run this test on your own life" — an invitation, not a rule.
- Values are self-administered by definition, so they *cannot* be prescribed; only duties can. Perhaps the shift to prescription is exactly the birth of the duty layer, and the answer is: the content did change — a second layer was added — and the values themselves did not.

> [!claude]
> Watch for a smuggled resolution here too: "it's just an invitation" is a comfortable answer that may let the framework avoid saying anything binding. The VC critique in Part 8 is not an invitation; it is a judgement. The philosophy already prescribes.

## A third option: description

The transcripts offer a reading that is neither report nor prescription:

> [!claude] From the political-economy session
> You've built a philosophy of symmetry. Tension held consciously. Strength without domination. Sovereignty that is only real when it's mutual. And you've described your own life in exactly those terms — the handwriting, the 15-year manuscript, the refusal to commoditise, the explicit acceptance that the reward arrives after you're gone. **You are already living the philosophy. The book is a description of what you're already doing.**

And the consequence drawn from it:

> [!claude]
> Your philosophy doesn't primarily need to be argued for. It needs to be **demonstrated** — in how you conduct yourself, advise businesses, raise whatever comes next. The manuscript matters; the living of it matters more. The manuscript only lands because the life behind it is real.

If that is right, the creed/prescription dichotomy is a false one. The content is *descriptive* — a report of what holding these tensions actually did — and the prescriptive force comes not from the text but from whether the life is legible and whether following it visibly pays. That routes the question into [[Theory of Change]] and into [[OQ-15 The Hidden Premise About Human Nature]], and it makes [[Part 9 — The Professional]] load-bearing rather than applied.

> [!claude]
> The obvious objection, kept here so it isn't lost: "demonstration, not argument" is an even more comfortable escape hatch than "invitation, not rule". It exempts the text from having to defend anything. The VC critique in Part 8 is still a judgement about other people's businesses, and no amount of living well makes it not one.

Compare with the structural-deference answer arrived at from a different direction in [[OQ-16 Morality Across Scales and the Blind Side]] — *a proposal, not a verdict*. Three independent arrivals at roughly the same shape is either [[Convergence as Evidence]] or three versions of the same flinch.
