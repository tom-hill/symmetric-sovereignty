# Part 11 — Money, Time and the Commons

> [!claude]
> **This part is proposed, not settled.** It exists because the single largest dialogue in the corpus — 445 lines on money, debt, deficits, democratic short-termism and education — had no home anywhere in the vault. The material is substantial and it is the civilisational scale of the anti-normalisation argument, which [[Part 7 — Scales]] promises and does not deliver. Tom to accept the part, fold it into Parts 7 and 8, or reject it.

## What it argues

The framework's critique of business ([[Part 8 — The Model for Modern Business is Broken]]) is a special case. The general case is about **capital optimising for a horizon shorter than the thing it is funding**, and it runs at civilisational scale through public finance.

Three moves:

1. **Money is a social agreement, not a stock.** Most of it is created by banks at the moment of lending; one party's debt is another's savings; the household-budget metaphor is false and load-bearing in policy. → [[Money as Social Agreement]]
2. **The real constraint is resources, and chiefly people.** Which makes a flattened population a poor one, and connects the economic argument directly to [[Variance and the Average That Doesn't Exist]]. → [[Real Resources Constraint]]
3. **Democracies under-invest by architecture.** Electoral cycles against 15–30 year payback; visible costs against diffuse benefits; austerity legible where complexity is not; and policy churn as its own failure mechanism. → [[Structural Short-termism]]

The philosophical payload: this is [[Normalisation]] operating through a **single scalar** at the largest available scale, a norm authored elsewhere, imposed, and with the exit closed — which makes it the best available worked example of [[Foreclosure]] and the answer to [[OQ-08 Why the Obsession]].

## Material (Tom's proposals, recorded not endorsed)
- A sovereign fund held at the Bank of England; political salaries continue to be paid from tax.
- Removal of first-past-the-post, so no government governs without a majority.
- **Remuneration tied to long-horizon outcomes**, so that losing the next cycle does not remove a party's interest in a policy surviving — and backtracking a predecessor's policy risks the backtracker's own payout.
- Education reform as the hardest change, attempted either by building an augmenting system locally and visibly until it is adopted, or by *frontal attack* — challenging the government publicly on why financial and civic literacy is not taught, and naming the self-interest in keeping it untaught.
