# Part 5 — Reversibility and Exit

## Argument (draft)

Some principles are chosen. Others are *found*, by noticing that they keep turning up. Reversibility is the second kind.

In the course of working the framework through — values, duties, business, normalisation, the professional — the same requirement kept appearing, unbidden, from different directions: that a legitimate exercise of sovereignty must leave a way back. The notebook's critique of venture capital is, at bottom, a critique of irreversibility — funding that "prolongs the death of companies" and "makes them brittle" locks founders into a path they cannot exit. The value [[Commitment without Rigidity]] is about holding a goal while keeping the ability to let it go — "a life cannot be lived by gripping it too tightly." [[Vulnerability without Collapse]] is about being open without being trapped by the openness. The duty layer needs a first duty, and the most defensible candidate is *do not trap*. And the practice of change leadership — making change survivable for the people inside it — is precisely the practice of not cutting off retreat.

Five arrivals. The framework's own method treats that kind of convergence as evidence: when a principle arrives independently from multiple directions, it is probably load-bearing rather than decorative. → [[Convergence as Evidence]]

So: **the legitimacy of any exercise of power over another — by a person, a company, an investor, a state — depends on whether the other can leave.** Sovereignty that removes exit is domination wearing a different coat.
