# Commitment without Rigidity

## Definition (Tom, notebook)
> Applying intense dedication to your goals and responsibilities whilst moving with the flow of the process rather than forcing it. A process cannot be understood by stopping it — and a life cannot be lived by gripping it too tightly.

Source: [[Spread D — Vulnerability, Commitment, Optimisation]]

## Reading
The value quotes Herbert directly ("a process cannot be understood by stopping it") and extends him: *a life cannot be lived by gripping it too tightly*. Commitment here is not persistence toward a fixed end but dedication that is willing to be changed by the process it is committed to. → [[Process Philosophy]]

## The shadow
**Rigidity** — forcing the process; gripping.


## The wind, 2026-10-02

Asked whether *"I know I will find my wind"* was a slip for *way*:

> [!question] Tom
> It is meant as second wind, the guiding force that pushes me (the boat) in a direction, even where the direction is yet unknown or not chosen. You do not control the wind (the direction), you harness it, and use it.

This is the mechanism the value has always needed. *Moving with the flow of the process rather than forcing it* says what to do and not how. **Harnessing** says how: you do not choose the force, you do not fight it, and you do not wait for it to be the one you wanted. You rig for it and you go.

> [!claude]
> It also fills a gap in the value set that had not been named. [[Agency without Domination]] is agency with respect to **other people**. [[Proactive Reactivity]] is preparation with respect to **time** — discipline upstream buying freedom downstream. Neither covers agency with respect to **circumstance**: forces that are neither yours nor anyone's, that you cannot negotiate with and cannot refuse.
>
> The boat is the right shape for it, and it is more precise than it first looks. A boat under sail is not carried. It holds a tension between wind and keel, and that tension is the only reason it can travel anywhere other than downwind. Remove either force and it drifts. That is the whole philosophy in a working object, and it arrived while Tom was describing being lost rather than while anyone was working a question.
>
> Worth testing against [[Reversibility and Exit]]: harnessing a force you did not choose is only safe while you can luff, or come about, or drop the sail. Harnessing without that is being driven.

> [!claude] The line then inverted, and it improved
> Tom first wrote *"I know I will find my wind"* and changed it to **"I know the next wind will find me."**
>
> That separates two things the first version ran together. **Arrival** is not his: he does not go looking, which is the whole argument of the piece and the thing he says he is trying to resist doing. **Use** still is: harnessing is what happens once it gets here. The seeking disappears and the agency does not.
>
> It is also the more honest claim. *I will find my wind* is a promise about his own effort, made by someone who has just admitted he does not know which direction to put effort in. *The next wind will find me* is a statement of trust in a process, which is what [[Process Philosophy]] asks for and what the value is named after.

→ [[On being lost]]
