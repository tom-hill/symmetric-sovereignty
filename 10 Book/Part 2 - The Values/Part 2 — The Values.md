# Part 2 — The Values

## The grammar

Every value in Symmetric Sovereignty takes the form **"X without Y."** X is a strength — something worth developing, possessing, and exercising. Y is what X becomes when it stops holding its tension: its shadow. The value is not X. The value is *X held against Y* — the strength kept in the condition where it protects and elevates rather than dominates and flattens.

This is why the values cannot be reduced to a list of virtues. "Strength" alone is not a value in this framework; neither is "kindness". The value is the *holding*. → [[Tension]], [[Shadow (Inversion)]]

The values are **self-administered**. Nobody can hold your tension for you and nobody can be made to hold it. This is what distinguishes them from the duties (Part 4), which are owed to others and bind regardless. → [[Values vs Duties]]

## Where the grammar strains

> [!claude]
> Three structural observations, offered for Tom to accept or reject:
> 1. **Not every Y is an inversion.** Cruelty is what strength *flips into*; suppression is what optimisation *gradually becomes*. If the values fail in two different ways, the grammar hides it. → [[OQ-01 Inversion vs Dosage Risks]]
> 2. **Two entries aren't "X without Y".** Proactive Reactivity is a compound noun; Tension without Immotion is arguably the thesis of Part 1 restated. Tom noticed both in the margin. The set may be seven values plus one principle plus one thesis.
> 3. **The "faces of one value" question may resolve upward.** Strength, Agency, and Leadership may be one value — *power without control* — expressed at three scales (self, other, system). If so, the scales framework (Part 7) is already inside the values.
