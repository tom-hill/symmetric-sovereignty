# Part 2 — The Values

## The grammar, and what it hides

Every value in Symmetric Sovereignty takes the form **"X without Y."** Strength without cruelty. Agency without domination. Read naturally, the formula says that X is the good thing, Y is what X becomes when it goes wrong, and the value is holding the first against the second.

That reading is wrong, and the formula is what made it hard to see. → [[OQ-01 Inversion vs Dosage Risks]]

**X is not the thing being valued. X is a capacity, and capacities are morally neutral.**

Strength in any volume does not create cruelty. Agency in any volume does not create domination. Optimisation in any quantity does not suppress anything. You can hold an enormous amount of any of them and harm nobody, and you can hold very little and do real damage. Nothing follows from the amount.

What follows is from the **direction of application** — and that is a term the formula never names. The whole moral content of every one of these values sits in a variable the sentence leaves out.

So the values are not strengths held against their shadows. They are **neutral capacities under a standing requirement about where they are pointed.**

Two kinds of direction, which behave differently and should not be run together:

- **Other-regarding.** Strength, agency and leadership are turned toward the people affected, or they are turned toward the self. That is the whole of it.
- **Reality-regarding.** Commitment and optimisation are turned toward new information and breadth of purpose, or away from them. Rigidity is a refusal of the world; a narrowed objective is the same refusal wearing a spreadsheet.

This is why the values cannot be reduced to a list of virtues — but not for the reason previously given. It is not that the value is the *holding*. It is that **the named thing is not the value at all.**

## How a value fails

Not by inversion, and not by dosage. **By misdirection.**

A value fails at the moment its capacity is turned toward the self rather than toward the people it affects, or away from the world rather than toward it. There is no threshold and no gradient. There is no amount of strength that tips into cruelty. There is a choice about what the strength is for, and it is remade continuously.

With one exception, and the exception is the important part.

**[[Reflection without Paralysis]] cannot be misdirected.** You cannot reflect *at* anyone. There is no direction to get wrong, and its failure really is volumetric: reflection accumulates until it substitutes for the action it was supposed to sharpen, and the threshold differs from person to person.

That single difference yields the rule that separates this part from Part 4:

> **A misdirection is observable by the counterparty. A volume is not.**

Someone can tell you that you used your strength to dominate them, that you narrowed an objective at their expense, that you refused information they handed you. **Nobody can tell you that you have reflected too much**, because the threshold is inside you and it is yours.

→ [[Part 4 — Duties, the Philosophical Constitution]]

The values are **self-administered**. Nobody can hold your tension for you and nobody can be made to hold it. This is what distinguishes them from the duties (Part 4), which are owed to others and bind regardless. → [[Values vs Duties]]

## Three capacities, one value

Tom's margin question beside the first three — *is there actually a differentiation between these 3, or are they all different faces of the same value?* — has an answer, and it is that both options were wrong. → [[OQ-05 Three Faces or One Value]]

They are not three values. They are not faces of one. They are **three capacities governed by one value**: power over capability, over the self, and over systems, under a single requirement that each be turned outward rather than inward.

Which explains the thing that made the question worth asking. The three feel redundant because the orientation is shared. Their shadows feel entirely distinct because **the shadow is named after the capacity, not after the failure.** Cruelty, domination and entitlement are three different experiences for the person receiving them precisely because three different capacities are being pointed at them — by one and the same failure.

## Where the grammar strains

> [!claude]
> Three structural observations, offered for Tom to accept or reject:
> 1. **Not every Y is an inversion.** Cruelty is what strength *flips into*; suppression is what optimisation *gradually becomes*. If the values fail in two different ways, the grammar hides it. → [[OQ-01 Inversion vs Dosage Risks]]
> 2. **Two entries aren't "X without Y".** Proactive Reactivity is a compound noun; Tension without Immotion is arguably the thesis of Part 1 restated. Tom noticed both in the margin. The set may be seven values plus one principle plus one thesis.
> 3. **The "faces of one value" question may resolve upward.** Strength, Agency, and Leadership may be one value — *power without control* — expressed at three scales (self, other, system). If so, the scales framework (Part 7) is already inside the values.
