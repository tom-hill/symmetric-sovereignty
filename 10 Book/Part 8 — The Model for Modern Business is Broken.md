# Part 8 — The Model for Modern Business is Broken

*This part exists in Tom's hand. The text below is the notebook page, lightly paragraphed; the marginalia follow. Source: [[Spread B — The Model for Modern Business is Broken]].*

## Argument (Tom, from the notebook)

The model for modern business is broken.

Venture Capital was designed as a way to seed ideas that are capital intensive in order to enable wider innovation and participation — e.g. creating new drugs.

It has now become the default way to build businesses as it became a business model of its own. It no longer serves the greater good by enabling others. It now serves as a self-cannibalising model focused on profit for its own [sake] and the LPs who fund them.

Modern funding prolongs the death of companies who did not serve a need. Sometimes long enough they do survive. But do so in a way that makes them brittle, and that sacrifices the sovereignty of the people who found them.

This model has led to entrepreneur as a *job*, filled by people chasing wealth not impact. Not exemplified more than the concept of "start-up" incubators who generate ideas for people to execute and they fund.

We have turned businesses themselves into commodity products.

We have lost the art of business, the type that was generational, passionate, and impact focused. We have indexed too heavily on money and valuation.
