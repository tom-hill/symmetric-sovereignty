## Argument

> [!blank]
> **This part is the book's honesty test.** The first Herbert quote is a warning about exactly the method being used to write the book. The framework's own answer is probably already in the material: sovereignty over one's thinking is not *doing all the thinking alone*, it is *retaining authorship* — the machine may pose questions, but the values are self-administered and the duties are owed by Tom, not by the tool. The vault's own convention (Claude's voice always marked, never merged) is a small structural version of that answer. Tom to decide whether that is sufficient or whether the danger is subtler — that a thinking partner which is *good at questions* shapes which questions get asked.

> [!blank]
> **The second quote as method.** "A process cannot be understood by stopping it" is why the philosophy is built inductively, session by session, in a notebook, rather than designed top-down. It is also why the manuscript will resist being finished. Is there a version of "done" that is consistent with process philosophy? (A *baseline*, perhaps — which is what this vault is.)
