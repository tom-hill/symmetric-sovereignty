# Part 0 — Why This Exists

## Material (stated)
- The philosophy is built **inductively from lived experience** — upward from a life, not downward from theory. → [[Inductive Method]]
- Part of that life: a childhood of excelling across domains while belonging to no social group, and a drive to prove capability.
- Symmetric Sovereignty is framed in part as a claim that **minds that don't fit standard categories are not broken**.
- It is a 10–15 year (or longer) project, built through handwritten notebooks, intended eventually as a manuscript.
- The fractional practice ([[Reyor]]) is a funded bridge while this takes shape — and also its test-bed.
- A private journal is kept separate from the philosophy notebook, so personal reflection and philosophical work don't contaminate each other.
- Claude is used as an impartial thinking partner — a choice that the philosophy itself has to be able to justify. → [[OQ-04 The Herbert Question]]

## Argument

> [!blank]
> **The preface is Tom's to write.** The material above says *what* the origin is; the preface needs to say why a reader should trust a philosophy that admits it started as one person's attempt to make sense of not fitting. The strongest version probably turns that admission into the method: the framework is offered as a report from one life, tested against others, and the reader is invited to run the same test (see [[OQ-02 Creed to Prescription]]).

### Prompts for the blank
- What is the earliest memory of *holding* a tension rather than resolving it?
- When did "I don't fit" stop being a wound and start being data?
- What would make this book *fail* on its own terms? (A philosophy of sovereignty that ends up as a set of rules would be one answer.)
