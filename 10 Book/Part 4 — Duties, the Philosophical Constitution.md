# Part 4 — Duties, the Philosophical Constitution

## Material (stated)
- The philosophy has sharpened into **two structural layers**:
  - **Values** — held in tension, *self-administered*. You hold them for yourself; nobody can hold them for you, and nobody can be made to hold them.
  - **Duties** — *owed to a counterparty*, and **binding on non-believers**. They form a "philosophical constitution".
- The distinction arrived in dialogue and is treated as a major sharpening. → [[Thread — Values and Duties]]
- [[Reversibility and Exit]] is a strong candidate for the first duty: the obligation to leave the other party a way out.
- "Sovereignty is legitimate only when it preserves others' sovereignty" reads as a duty, not a value. → [[Sovereignty]]

## The selection rule, 2026-10-28

The two layers were asserted before anything said which values belonged in which. [[OQ-01 Inversion vs Dosage Risks]] supplies the rule, and it falls out of how values actually fail rather than being declared.

Values fail by **misdirection**: a neutral capacity turned toward the self rather than toward the people it affects. One value fails differently — [[Reflection without Paralysis]] cannot be pointed anywhere, and fails by sheer volume instead.

> **A misdirection is observable by the counterparty. A volume is not.**

Someone can tell you that you used your strength to dominate them, narrowed an objective at their expense, or refused the information they handed you. They were there. They are holding the consequence. **Nobody can tell you that you have reflected too much**, because that threshold is internal and individual.

So:

- **Directed failures can become duties.** There is a counterparty with standing to invoke them, which is the thing [[Duties are Invoked]] requires and the thing that makes a duty binding rather than a private scruple.
- **Volumetric failures can only ever be values.** Self-administered by *necessity*, not by preference. Nobody outside has access to the measurement, so nobody outside can hold you to it.

This also answers why the layers divide where they do. It is not that values are personal and duties are social as a matter of taste. It is that **a duty requires an observer, and only some failures have one.**

> [!claude]
> Three consequences worth following up rather than leaving implicit.
>
> **The duty list is now constrained.** Every candidate in [[OQ-13 Enumerate the Duties]] can be tested: name who observes the failure and what they saw. Anything failing that test is a value in a coat.
>
> **The constitution is short for a reason.** Not as a stylistic preference but because few failures are externally observable. That is a better argument for minimalism than any appeal to elegance.
>
> **The rule is silent on binding.** It says which failures *could* be duties, not whether a duty binds an individual or a collective. That remains [[OQ-18 The Unit of Revisability]] and still waits.

## Argument

> [!blank]
> **The duties themselves are not yet enumerated.** This is the largest blank in the manuscript. The dialogue established that the layer exists and what distinguishes it; it did not (as far as the record shows) list the duties. Candidates implied by the rest of the material:
> - The duty to preserve the other's sovereignty (never to standardise, control, or override another's agency — from [[Agency without Domination]])
> - The duty of reversibility — to build exits, never to trap ([[Reversibility and Exit]])
> - The duty of honesty about fragility ([[Vulnerability without Exemption]] — "honest about your fragility")
> - The duty not to displace — to add without crowding out ([[Additive vs Displacing Forces]])
> - The duty to share capability rather than goods ([[Capability-sharing vs Goods-sharing]])
>
> Tom to confirm which of these are duties (owed, binding) versus values (held, self-administered), and which are missing.

> [!blank]
> **Why "binding on non-believers"?** This is the claim that makes the layer a *constitution* rather than a creed — and the claim that most needs defending. On what authority does a duty bind someone who rejects the philosophy? Possible answers: (a) it doesn't bind them, it binds *me* in how I treat them, regardless of whether they reciprocate — which makes duties asymmetric and is philosophically cleaner; (b) it binds them because it's the precondition of any shared system at all — a Hobbesian/contractarian route; (c) something else. The record doesn't settle this. → [[OQ-02 Creed to Prescription]]

> [!claude]
> Note the tension (appropriately) between the two layers: values are radically individual and duties are radically relational. That's not a flaw — it looks like the framework's own principle applied to itself. Worth saying so explicitly in this part.
