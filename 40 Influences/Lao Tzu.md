# Lao Tzu

**Read the *Tao Te Ching*. Deceptively short, inexhaustibly deep.**

## What he gives the framework
*Wu wei* — acting in accordance with natural process rather than forcing it — rhymes almost exactly with [[Commitment without Rigidity]] and with the Herbert line about moving with the flow of the process. Water as the model: soft, yielding, and it carves canyons. **Leadership without domination is substantially a Daoist idea**, and arrives here from outside the Western lineage entirely, which makes it evidence rather than echo. → [[Convergence as Evidence]]

## Where he pushes back
Straight into [[Cyclical vs Progressive Time]]. The Daoist frame is one of return and maintenance, not growth and emergence. Tension does not go anywhere; it turns. If that is right, "tension is generative" has a progress metaphysics smuggled inside it that the framework has never argued for.

Also worth sitting with: Daoism is suspicious of articulation itself — "the Tao that can be told is not the eternal Tao". A philosophy built in notebooks has to take that seriously. → [[OQ-16 Morality Across Scales and the Blind Side]]
