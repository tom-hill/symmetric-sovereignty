# Tocqueville

**Read *Democracy in America*, the chapters on soft despotism.**

## What he gives the framework
The mechanism. Tocqueville saw that democratic societies produce conformity **not through force but through social pressure and the need for external validation** — citizens reduced to "timid and industrious animals of which government is the shepherd". That is [[Normalisation]] operating exactly as the framework describes, identified in 1840, and it is close to a description of the algorithmic present.

Crucially for [[OQ-14 Normalisation as the Only Unheld Tension]], soft despotism is *chosen*. Nobody imposes it. That makes it the hardest case for the authorship test in [[Authorship vs Imposition]] — a norm can be endogenous and still flatten.

## Where he pushes back
He thought the counterweight was **associational life** — churches, clubs, local institutions — rather than individual sovereignty. If he is right, the framework's answer is at the wrong level: the remedy for flattening is thicker intermediate institutions, not stronger selves. That routes straight into [[Relational vs Bounded Self]].
