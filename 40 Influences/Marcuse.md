# Marcuse

**Read *One-Dimensional Man*. Harder going than the rest of the [[Reading List]] and directly relevant.**

## What he gives the framework
The claim that advanced industrial society does not suppress opposition but **absorbs and neutralises** it — standardising desire itself, so that people want what the system needs them to want. That is [[Normalisation]] at its most complete: not a norm imposed on a self, but a self manufactured to fit the norm.

It is also the sharpest challenge to [[Genuine Wants]]. If desire is manufactured, "a genuine want requires no self-persuasion" is not a reliable instrument — the manufactured want would also arrive without argument. The framework needs an answer to that, and does not have one.

## Where he pushes back
Marcuse's diagnosis leaves very little room for individual sovereignty as a remedy, because the individual is precisely what has been colonised. His answer is collective and political. The framework's is individual and relational. One of them is wrong about where the leverage is.
