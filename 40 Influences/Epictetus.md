# Epictetus

**Read the *Enchiridion* first, then the *Discourses*.**

## What he gives the framework
[[Agency without Domination]] arrived at from the opposite direction. Epictetus was a slave. He had no external agency whatsoever, and built total internal sovereignty from that position — the dichotomy of control: absolute authority over your inner life, acceptance of everything outside it.

That matters because it separates **sovereignty over one's own thinking** from any material precondition. If the framework's first value requires wealth, standing, or freedom to exercise, it is a philosophy for the comfortable. Epictetus is the proof that it is not.

## Where he pushes back
The dichotomy of control is a *resolution*, not a held tension. Epictetus does not hold the opposition between what he can and cannot change — he severs it and discards one side. If that severance works, it is a counterexample to the claim that resolution produces imbalance. The framework should either absorb it or say why the case is different. → [[Part 1 — Tension as the Generative Condition]]
