# Mill

**Read *On Liberty*, Chapter 3 — "Of Individuality, as One of the Elements of Well-Being".**

## What he gives the framework
The argument that standardisation of human character is civilisational suicide **even under benevolent conditions** — that the diversity of human types is not a problem to be managed but the source of all progress. This is the strongest available defence of [[Variance and the Average That Doesn't Exist]], made a century and a half before the framework needed it.

Mill also supplies the harm principle, which is close to Tom's own line — *forceful deliberate harm to others* — and supplies the analytical defence of it that [[Creative vs Coercive Tension]] currently lacks.

## Where he pushes back
Mill is a utilitarian: individuality matters because it produces better outcomes in aggregate. The framework treats held tension as good in itself. If Mill is right, the whole argument is instrumental and can be overridden whenever the aggregate says otherwise — which is the opening [[Optimisation without Suppression]] is trying to close.
