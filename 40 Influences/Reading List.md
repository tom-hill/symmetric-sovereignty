# Reading List

*Recovered 2026-09-16 from the dialogue transcripts. [[Provenance]] previously recorded these contents as lost; they were not. Organised under Tom's four headings, with a fifth that the transcript adds. Reading order matters where noted.*

## Direct lineage — already implicit in the notebook

- **Heraclitus** — read *before* Nietzsche. Survives only in fragments, and they are the philosophical origin of the tension argument. "Opposition brings concord. Out of discord comes the fairest harmony." → [[Heraclitus]]
- **Nietzsche**, in order: *Thus Spoke Zarathustra* for the vision, *Beyond Good and Evil* for the argument, *On the Genealogy of Morality* for how values are constructed. *Will to power* is widely misread as domination; it is about self-overcoming. [[Strength without Cruelty]] is essentially a response to that misreading. → [[Nietzsche]]

## Process and flow

- **Henri Bergson** — *Creative Evolution*. *Duration* (lived time as flow, not measurable units) and *élan vital*. → [[Bergson]]
- **Lao Tzu** — *Tao Te Ching*. *Wu wei* — acting with natural process rather than forcing it — rhymes almost exactly with [[Commitment without Rigidity]] and the Herbert quote. Water as the model: soft, yielding, carves canyons. → [[Lao Tzu]]
- **Zhuangzi** — process philosophy lived rather than argued. The butterfly dream. → [[Zhuangzi]]

## Anti-normalisation

- **Ralph Waldo Emerson** — *Self-Reliance*, before almost anything else on this list. "Society everywhere is in conspiracy against the manhood of every one of its members." Writes the notebook's normalisation argument in 1841. → [[Emerson]]
- **John Stuart Mill** — *On Liberty*, specifically **Chapter 3, "Of Individuality, as One of the Elements of Well-Being"**. Standardisation of human character, even under benevolent conditions, as civilisational suicide. → [[Mill]]
- **Alexis de Tocqueville** — *Democracy in America*, the chapters on **soft despotism**. Conformity produced by social pressure rather than force; citizens as "timid and industrious animals of which government is the shepherd". → [[Tocqueville]]
- **Herbert Marcuse** — *One-Dimensional Man*. How advanced industrial society absorbs and neutralises opposition, standardising desire itself. → [[Marcuse]]

## Necessary challengers, and leadership

- **Marcus Aurelius** — *Meditations*. Read early. A man with absolute power writing privately about why absolute power is not the point. → [[Marcus Aurelius]]
- **Epictetus** — *Discourses*, and the shorter *Enchiridion*. [[Agency without Domination]] arrived at from the opposite direction: a former slave with no external agency who built total internal sovereignty. → [[Epictetus]]
- **Machiavelli** — *Discourses on Livy* **alongside** *The Prince*. The Discourses carry the real argument: republican virtue, earned legitimacy, what makes leadership sustainable. → [[Machiavelli]]
- **Isaiah Berlin** — on **value pluralism**. Added 2026-09-16 as the single most important challenger, because he is the one who forces the framework to account for people in a free society choosing, rationally and legitimately, differently than it hopes. → [[Isaiah Berlin]], [[OQ-15 The Hidden Premise About Human Nature]]

## Learning to write philosophy

- **Montaigne** — *Essays*. Invented the essay as a mode of thinking in public: personal, exploratory, full of contradiction. His method — thinking through experience toward principle rather than principle toward proof — is the model for what the notebooks are already doing. → [[Montaigne]]
- **Albert Camus** — *The Myth of Sisyphus*. Irreducible tension that cannot be resolved, only lived. Also worth studying for style: philosophical argument at the clarity and urgency of literature. → [[Camus]]

## Cases to go and find
Named in dialogue as evidence the framework needs and does not have:
- **The Nordic social model** — the most cited political approximation of distributed sovereignty. Examine honestly, including where it fails and which cultural preconditions do not transfer. Note the tension with [[Thread — Finding a Country]]: the Jante Law is why Scandinavia may fail this framework's test.
- **Mondragon** — the Basque worker-owned cooperative network that survived Franco and grew into a significant economy, built explicitly on economic sovereignty being symmetric. Smaller and more instructive than the Nordic case.
