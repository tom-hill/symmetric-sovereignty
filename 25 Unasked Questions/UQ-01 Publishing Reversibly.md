# UQ-01 — How do you publish a position reversibly?

## The sense
[[Reversibility and Exit]] is load-bearing. Publishing is the opposite: once a position is public, changing it costs something — reputationally, and more importantly psychologically, because defending what you said starts to feel like integrity.

A philosophy that makes exit a duty owes itself an account of how its own author exits a published position.

## What triggered it
Deciding, on 2026-09-16, to publish openly as the work proceeds. It became live the moment the decision was made rather than in the abstract.

## What it sits next to
[[Reversibility and Exit]] · [[Publishing is not Marketing]] · [[Foreclosure]] — a published position is arguably a norm you have authored and then closed the exit on, for yourself

## Possible shapes it might take
- Is there a *form* of publishing that stays reversible? Publishing questions rather than conclusions might be exactly that — which would make Tom's instinct to publish the open questions structurally correct rather than merely honest.
- Is the version history of a public repo a reversibility mechanism, or the opposite — a permanent record of every position ever held?
- Does the framework distinguish *changing your mind* from *exit*? Exit is leaving a system. Changing your mind is staying and revising. They may need different words.
