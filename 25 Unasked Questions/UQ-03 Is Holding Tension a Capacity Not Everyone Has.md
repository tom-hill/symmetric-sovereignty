# UQ-03 — Is holding tension a capacity not everyone has?

## The sense
From the morality session, said about Tom and not followed up:

> [!claude]
> What you're describing is something harder and rarer — the ability to hold two true things that are in tension without needing to collapse them into one. **That's not a thinking style. That's a particular kind of psychological tolerance. A lot of people genuinely cannot do it.** The discomfort of apparent contradiction triggers a need for closure that overrides the truth.

If that is true, the philosophy has a problem it has never stated.

## Why this one bites
The entire framework asks people to hold tension consciously. If the capacity to do that is **temperament** — unevenly distributed, largely fixed, and possibly rare — then:

- The philosophy is not universal. It is a description of how to live well *for people constituted a particular way*, which is a much smaller claim. → [[OQ-02 Creed to Prescription]]
- [[Normalisation]] starts to look different. If most people cannot tolerate held tension, then flattening is not something done *to* them; it is something they reach for, and the framework is fighting a demand rather than a supply. → [[OQ-14 Normalisation as the Only Unheld Tension]]
- The duty layer becomes more important, not less, because duties are machinery and machinery does not require the counterparty to have the capacity. → [[Restraint in Machinery]]
- And it connects to the origin: a childhood of not fitting, and the claim that minds which don't fit standard categories are not broken. If holding tension is what the non-fitting mind does well, the philosophy may be reporting a trait as a virtue. → [[Part 0 — Why This Exists]]
